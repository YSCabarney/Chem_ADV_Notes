Gold Bar Detectives

Eight vault samples just came in. Gold is trading at record highs, and someone may be passing off fake bars. Your group has 14 minutes to run the numbers and decide what's real.

30 mintotal class time
8students, 4 Zoom breakout pairs
Mass ÷ Volumeonly tool required

Why this activity

It's a real 2026 story, and your notes already gave students the tool to investigate it

With gold prices at record highs this year, counterfeiters have been drilling out real gold bars, packing the core with tungsten, and resealing them with a thin layer of real gold. A bathroom-scale-style weight check can't catch it, because tungsten's density (19.25 g/cm³) sits almost exactly on top of gold's (19.30 g/cm³). Refiners and dealers have had to build multi-step verification protocols just to catch it.

Students already have the exact tool that starts this investigation: density = mass ÷ volume. This activity hands them a stack of "vault samples" and asks them to be the first line of defense — then reveals that one sample is the same trap that's fooling professionals right now.

Run of show

Timing for a 30-minute period

  • 0–4 min Frame the case

    Share the headline live: record gold prices, real tungsten-core counterfeits, refiners losing money. Ask: "If a fake bar can weigh the same as a real one, how would you even start checking it?" Take 2–3 guesses before revealing the tool: density.
  • 4–6 min Set up the tool + assign rooms

    Put the formula on screen: density = mass ÷ volume. Send pairs into 4 breakout rooms with the case file (below). Each pair gets all 4 bars.
  • 6–20 min Breakout investigation

    Pairs calculate density for all 4 bars, compare to the reference table, and label each bar Genuine, Fake, or Can't tell from density alone. They prep one sentence of justification per bar.
  • 20–27 min Report back

    Each pair reports their verdict on Bar D specifically. Expect a split room — that's the point. Let two pairs argue it out before you reveal why.
  • 27–30 min Reveal + bridge to next class

    Confirm Bar D is intentionally built to mirror the real tungsten trick — density alone can't resolve it. Preview that next class covers what actually catches it (and formally introduces density as a named property).

Answer key

If a group insists Bar D is "probably fine"

Push on it: what test, if any, could break the tie? This is a good moment to let them sit in the discomfort of "the math checks out and it still might be fake" — that tension is the whole reason this is a live investigative problem for the precious metals industry right now, not a solved one.

Debrief prompts

Closing the loop

  • Which bars were easy calls, and why did density alone settle them?
  • What made Bar D different from the other three?
  • If you ran a bank vault, would you trust density as your only check? What else might you want to test?

This sets up density as a named, defined property next class — and gives you a real, current hook to return to when you formally introduce the density formula and units (g/cm³, g/mL).
