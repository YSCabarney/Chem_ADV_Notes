Why a Turkey Fryer Explodes

Six steps of chemistry turn a frozen turkey and a pot of oil into a house fire.

Covalent bonds→Polar vs nonpolar→Way past boiling→Steam ×1,600→Combustion→Fire spreads
1

Meet the cast

All three are nonmetals. They share electrons, so every bond in water and oil is covalent.

Oxygen needs 2 more electrons for an octet, so it bonds with 2 hydrogens: H₂O.

2

One polar, one not

O–H Δ 1.24POLAR C–H Δ 0.35NONPOLAR

Like dissolves like, so the water won't mix into the oil. It's denser, so it sinks.

3

Way past boiling

Oil is 138°F hotter than water's boiling point. The water doesn't simmer. It flashes to steam all at once.

4

Steam needs room

Steam takes up about 1,600 times the space of liquid water. The gas rockets up and throws hot oil over the rim.

5

Oil becomes fuel

oil (C, H) + O₂
→ CO₂ + H₂O + heat

Spilled oil hits the burner. Combustion breaks covalent bonds and builds new ones, releasing energy.

Black smoke = soot, unburned carbon from a fire short on oxygen.

6

Location matters

The fryer sat on a wooden deck against the house. More fuel let the fire spread and climb the wall.

What would have helped, and why

DO

Thaw, dry, fry outside, far from the house. No water in the pot, no steam blast.

NEVER

Water on a grease fire. It sinks, flashes to steam, and sprays burning oil everywhere.

IONIC HEROES

NaHCO₃, NaCl, or a Class K (K⁺) extinguisher can smother a small grease fire.

BIG FIRE

Past smothering, like the one in the video. Get away and call 911.
