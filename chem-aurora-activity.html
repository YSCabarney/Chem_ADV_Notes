Starlight Detectives

Scientists have never touched most of the planets and stars they study — some are so far away their light takes years to reach us. So how do they know what those worlds are made of? Today, you'll crack that case.

A Planet We'll Never Visit

⏱ 3 min

Over the past few years, space telescopes have been announcing something wild: they've identified the exact gases inside the atmospheres of exoplanets — planets orbiting stars other than our sun, so far away their light takes years to reach Earth. Scientists have found water vapor, carbon dioxide, even methane on worlds humans will probably never set foot on.

How light from another world reaches us

As the planet crosses in front of its star, a sliver of starlight filters straight through the planet's atmosphere before reaching our telescopes — picking up a chemical "fingerprint" along the way.

Your first guess — no wrong answers

Before we explain anything: how do you think scientists could possibly know what gases are in a planet's atmosphere without ever going there?

2

What's Actually Happening

⏱ 2 min

Here's the short version — we'll go deeper over the next few classes:

Splitting starlight into a spectrum

A prism (or a spectrograph, its high-tech cousin) spreads starlight into a rainbow. Gases in the way soak up their own specific colors, leaving dark gaps — a fingerprint of exactly which gases the light passed through.

🔭

Every gas — whether it's floating in a star, wrapped around a planet, or drifting through an exoplanet's atmosphere light years away — absorbs and releases the exact same colors every time. That consistency is what makes this whole trick work.

4

Sneak Peek: the Words Behind the Light

⏱ 4 min

You don't need to memorize these yet — just tap each card to meet the four ideas we'll build the whole unit on.

⚛️ Atom
The tiny particle every gas is made of — whether it's floating in a lab flame or drifting through an exoplanet's atmosphere. Every spectral line you matched in Step 3 starts inside one of these.
⚡ Electron
A tiny negatively-charged particle inside every atom. When starlight or heat energy hits an atom, it's the electron that absorbs it.
🪜 Energy Level
Electrons can only sit at specific "steps" of energy — never in between. Absorb energy and an electron jumps up a step. It can't stay there long.
✨ Photon
When the electron falls back down a step, it releases the extra energy as a single particle of light — a photon. Its color depends on exactly how big that step was, which is why every element's fingerprint is unique.
